# Analytics Framework Glossary

This glossary explains public-safe concepts used in the Sentiment Analytics Dashboard. These terms are intended for communication, documentation, and dashboard interpretation. They do not reveal private scoring formulas or production implementation details.

## Attention

A measure of how much public participation or engagement exists around an asset or topic.

Attention can rise even when sentiment is mixed or negative. High attention does not automatically mean a narrative is correct or investable.

## Validation

A stronger concept than attention. Validation means the surrounding context appears to support or confirm a narrative.

Public-safe rule:

> Attention is not the same thing as validation.

## Sentiment

A directional tone measure that summarizes whether public discussion is leaning positive, negative, or neutral.

Sentiment is useful context, but it should not be treated as a standalone trading signal.

## Narrative pressure

A broad phrase for the combination of sentiment direction, attention intensity, and market context around an asset.

Narrative pressure can be constructive, deteriorating, mixed, or noisy.

## Participation breadth

A measure of whether discussion appears broad-based or concentrated. Broad participation may imply a more widely distributed narrative, while narrow participation may imply a more fragile signal.

## Engagement quality

A qualitative way to describe the usefulness of attention. High engagement is not always high-quality engagement. The dashboard should distinguish between meaningful participation and noisy spikes where possible.

## Sentiment repair

A situation where sentiment begins improving after a negative or stressed period. This does not imply price will move in a specific direction. It simply describes a change in public tone.

## Divergence

A condition where sentiment, attention, or narrative behavior appears to move differently from market context.

Example:

> Price strength with deteriorating sentiment may suggest weakening narrative support.

## Regime context

The broader environment in which sentiment and market behavior are interpreted. A sentiment reading may mean different things during high-volatility, low-volatility, risk-on, or risk-off environments.

## Public-safe summary

The glossary should help reviewers understand how the system communicates behavioral market context while avoiding financial advice, price targets, guarantees, or buy/sell language.
